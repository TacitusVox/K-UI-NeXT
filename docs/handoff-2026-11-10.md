## FMV performance theory

- **Movie players use the audio as their clock.** When K-UI takes CPU time,
  or a read is slow, the picture falls behind while the audio stays correct.
  Armada VMU On proved this: a 14% hook made a slideshow, and under 1% fixed
  it.
- **Native CD-music games, Toy Commander most of all:**
  - The CD-music route has no background reader.
  - Toy's movie reads tiny pieces and waits for each one: 540 reads,
    about 38 ms each, first byte after about 11 ms. About 22 s of the intros
    are spent waiting, about 97% of it inside K-UI's reader.
  - The fix direction is sequential read-ahead on that route.
  - The deciding console test (not yet run): Toy with CD Music Off and
    Background On.

### For the next Toy change

1. Start from this branch: merge `claude/great-darwin-yjmgfi` into the Toy
   branch first.
2. Put a Toy-only change to a shared source under a Toy switch, or under
   `#if KUI_TOY_TEST_BUILD` with Main's text under `#else`. Then run
   `make test-toy-test-switches`. A deliberate production change is recorded
   with `--write`, named in its commit, and is its own behavior change under
   `AGENTS.md`.
3. A new Toy Makefile sets `-DKUI_TOY_TEST_BUILD=1` in `FLAGS` and
   `NATIVE_FLAGS`, as `Makefile.vmu-native-toy-movie` and
   `Makefile.vmu-native-toy-cache` do. The legacy resident drops the Toy
   switches from its flags and would otherwise compile Main's text.
4. Pin any new asm statement with more than three operands in
   `tests/test_asm_audit.py`, with the reason.
5. Give a check that needs build arguments a `main()`, so unittest discovery
   can import it.
6. A test that needs a report saves it to the SD card, also from a Games
   launch. Do not ask the owner to photograph report pages.
